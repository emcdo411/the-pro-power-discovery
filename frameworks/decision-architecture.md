# Decision Architecture

## Purpose

Decision architecture defines how operational signals become leadership decisions, field actions, and measurable outcomes. For Pro Power Support, the decision architecture should connect power infrastructure work, field execution, proposal strategy, inspections, mobile deployment, government contracting, and AI-supported workflows into one operating logic.

## Core Principle

A company does not become more intelligent because it has more software. It becomes more intelligent when the right signal reaches the right owner at the right time with the right action path.

## Decision Flow Model

### 1. Signal Capture

Operational signals may come from field technicians, inspection reports, mobile unit status, equipment failure events, customer requests, RFP documents, bid requirements, maintenance schedules, dispatch updates, vendor constraints, leadership observations, and AI tool outputs.

### 2. Signal Classification

Each signal should be classified by decision type: emergency response, maintenance priority, proposal risk, contract compliance, equipment availability, staffing constraint, customer escalation, government contracting requirement, cost or margin concern, or AI recommendation requiring human validation.

### 3. Ownership Assignment

Every decision category needs a clear owner. Ownership should answer: who is accountable, who approves, who performs the action, who must be informed, and who verifies closure.

### 4. Trigger Definition

Triggers convert passive information into action. Examples: inspection finding repeats across three assets, mobile unit utilization drops below target, project delay exceeds threshold, AI-generated recommendation conflicts with field judgment.

### 5. Escalation Path

Escalation should be structured, not personality-driven. It should define when the issue escalates, who receives it, what evidence is required, what decision must be made, and how closure is verified.

### 6. Feedback Loop

Every decision should feed the operating model, updating proposal assumptions, maintenance planning, inspection criteria, scheduling logic, staffing requirements, equipment utilization strategy, and AI prompt/output validation rules.

## Decision Architecture Problems to Look For

- owner-dependent decision paths
- undocumented exception handling
- repeated issues treated as isolated events
- field updates that never become management insight
- AI output with no accountability pathway
- proposal assumptions disconnected from real execution data
- compliance requirements without evidence ownership

## Strategic Value

A stronger decision architecture allows Pro Power Support to reduce founder dependency, improve field-to-office trust, accelerate proposal response, strengthen government contracting readiness, and make AI useful inside actual operations instead of leaving it as a disconnected tool.
