# Friction Mapping

## Purpose

Friction Mapping identifies the operational points where work slows down, information loses fidelity, accountability becomes unclear, or AI tools fail to translate into useful action.

For Pro Power Support, friction is likely to appear between the field and office, between proposal intent and execution reality, between inspections and maintenance prioritization, and between AI output and operator trust.

## Friction Types

### Communication Friction

Symptoms: too many calls or texts required to confirm status, field updates not visible to leadership, customer urgency passed informally, operating truth changes faster than reports.

### Decision Friction

Symptoms: unclear authority, repeated owner involvement, decision stalls during exceptions, multiple people know part of the answer but no one owns the full decision.

### Data Friction

Symptoms: information exists but is hard to retrieve, reports show lagging status, inspection history cannot be analyzed quickly, proposal assumptions live in old documents or memory.

### Workflow Friction

Symptoms: manual handoffs, inconsistent intake, rework, duplicate entry, missing escalation rules.

### AI Friction

Symptoms: AI output is too generic, recommendations are not traceable, operators do not trust the result, output does not connect to a real task, model answers do not reflect current operational constraints.

## AI-Specific Friction Review

The current AI usage should be assessed against five questions:

1. What operational data can it actually access?
2. Does the output include evidence or just answers?
3. Does the output map to a real workflow?
4. Who validates the recommendation?
5. Does the accepted output update the operating system?

If these answers are weak, the issue is not the model. The issue is workflow architecture.

## Final Output

The final friction map should identify: top five workflow friction points, current cost of friction, required ownership correction, immediate low-lift interventions, AI opportunities that are safe to test, and AI opportunities that should wait until workflow clarity improves.
