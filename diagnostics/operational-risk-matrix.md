# Operational Risk Matrix

## Purpose

The Operational Risk Matrix ranks hidden execution risks across Pro Power Support's operating model. It is designed for leadership review, not technical analysis. The goal is to identify where risk is accumulating before it becomes downtime, contract exposure, margin loss, or emergency escalation.

## Scoring Method

| Score | Likelihood | Impact | Visibility | Ownership Clarity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Low | Clearly visible | Clear owner |
| 2 | Occasional | Moderate | Somewhat visible | Mostly clear |
| 3 | Likely | High | Partially hidden | Unclear in places |
| 4 | Frequent | Severe | Mostly hidden | Weak ownership |
| 5 | Active / recurring | Critical | Not visible until failure | No clear owner |

**Composite Risk Priority = Likelihood + Impact + Visibility + Ownership Clarity**

## Initial Risk Categories

| Risk Area | Description | Likelihood | Impact | Visibility | Ownership | Priority |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Field-to-office reporting lag | Leadership receives operational truth after delay | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 15 |
| Proposal assumption drift | Quotes rely on outdated labor, equipment, or material assumptions | 4 | 5 | 3 | 3 | 15 |
| Tribal knowledge concentration | Critical knowledge held by specific people rather than systems | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 17 |
| Mobile unit coordination risk | Unit status, crew availability, and customer urgency not unified | 3 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 15 |
| Inspection findings not trendable | Repeated findings treated as isolated events | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 16 |
| Government contract compliance gap | Requirements lack assigned evidence owner | 3 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 16 |
| AI output trust failure | AI recommendations lack source evidence, workflow fit, or owner validation | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 14 |
| Leadership decision dependency | Owner remains required for too many operational decisions | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 16 |
| Maintenance risk escalation | Equipment degradation does not trigger early executive visibility | 3 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 15 |
| Dispatch and scheduling friction | Work changes faster than planning visibility | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 14 |

## Priority Bands

| Score Range | Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 16 to 20 | Critical | Requires executive attention and ownership redesign |
| 12 to 15 | High | Requires workflow intervention and monitoring |
| 8 to 11 | Moderate | Watchlist item with periodic review |
| 4 to 7 | Low | Manage through existing process |

## Initial Executive Interpretation

The highest risks are not purely technical. They are operating-system risks: knowledge that lives inside people instead of systems, recurring field patterns that do not become strategic intelligence, proposal assumptions that drift away from execution reality, compliance requirements that lack evidence ownership, and owner dependency that limits scale.

## Recommended Next Step

Validate each risk through interviews and workflow evidence. The matrix should be updated after discovery with real examples, owners, triggers, and action paths.
