# Priority Action Stack

## Purpose

The Priority Action Stack converts the DARE assessment into an ordered set of actions. It is designed to help leadership decide what to fix first, what to test next, and what not to automate until the operating model is clearer.

## Executive Rule

Do not automate chaos. Stabilize the decision path first, then apply AI where it can reduce friction, improve visibility, or accelerate trusted action.

## Priority 1: Establish Operational Signal Inventory

**Objective:** Identify the core signals leadership needs to run the business with confidence.

Actions: list all critical operational signals, identify current source for each signal, identify whether signal is timely, reliable, and visible, assign decision owner for each signal category.

**Output:** Operational signal inventory and signal reliability score.

## Priority 2: Map the Five Highest-Value Decisions

**Objective:** Identify where delayed decisions create the largest financial, operational, or customer impact.

Candidate decisions: go / no-go on RFP or government bid, mobile unit deployment priority, inspection issue escalation, emergency response assignment, proposal pricing approval.

**Output:** Decision latency map with owners, triggers, and verification standards.

## Priority 3: Build RFP and Proposal Intelligence Workflow

**Objective:** Use AI where the business likely has immediate ROI — bid review, requirements extraction, proposal drafting, scope risk, and compliance tracking.

**Output:** RFP intake and proposal intelligence workflow.

## Priority 4: Create Inspection Trend and Maintenance Risk Review

**Objective:** Turn inspection activity into predictive operational intelligence.

Actions: standardize inspection finding categories, define severity levels, identify recurring issues, map findings by customer, asset, equipment class, location, and date, define escalation thresholds.

**Output:** Inspection intelligence and maintenance risk matrix.

## Priority 5: Fix AI Workflow Trust Before Expanding AI Use

**Objective:** Determine why the current AI layer is underperforming and redesign it around workflow trust.

Actions: audit current AI use cases, identify what data the AI can and cannot access, define acceptable AI outputs, require source evidence for operational recommendations, assign human validator by workflow, create accepted / revised / rejected feedback loop.

**Output:** AI workflow integration assessment and trust pathway.

## Priority 6: Reduce Leadership Decision Dependency

**Objective:** Move repeatable decisions away from owner bottlenecks without reducing control.

Actions: identify decisions only leadership currently makes, separate strategic decisions from operating decisions, assign clear operators for repeatable workflows, define escalation triggers, create weekly operating brief.

**Output:** Operator ownership model and executive exception dashboard.

## Priority 7: Build Weekly Operational Intelligence Brief

**Objective:** Give leadership a single weekly view of operational health.

Brief sections: top risks, delayed decisions, equipment constraints, proposal pipeline status, inspection trends, mobile unit utilization, AI recommendations requiring review, actions completed, actions stuck.

**Output:** Weekly operational intelligence brief template.

## 30-Day Execution Sequence

| Week | Focus | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | signal inventory + stakeholder interviews | signal map |
| 2 | decision latency + friction mapping | decision map |
| 3 | risk matrix + AI workflow review | risk and AI assessment |
| 4 | priority stack + executive brief | action plan |

## Success Criteria

- leadership can see where execution is drifting
- decisions have clear owners and triggers
- AI use cases are tied to real workflows
- proposal and RFP review becomes faster and safer
- inspection data begins producing trend intelligence
- leadership dependency decreases without weakening control
